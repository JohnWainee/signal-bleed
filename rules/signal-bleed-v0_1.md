# SIGNAL BLEED
### A rules-light game of chrome, neon, and the thing on the other side of the wire.
*Playtest draft v0.1 — the Neon Theorize engine*

---

## WHAT THIS IS

Three to five of you play burnouts, freelancers and true believers in a city that runs on other people's data. You take a job. The job is wrong. Something is bleeding through the network — something that was here before the network, and will be here after — and the only way to understand it is to look directly at it.

Looking at it changes you. That's the game.

**Nobody at the table knows the answer, including the GM.** The GM prepares a case: places, people, and clues. The *solution* does not exist until you invent it. When you assemble enough of the picture and speak it aloud, your theory becomes the truth of the world.

You will probably survive. You will not stay yourself.

**Play time:** 3–4 hours for one case. Runs as a one-shot or a series.
**You need:** a GM, 3–5 players, two six-sided dice, index cards, pencils.

---

## THE CORE ROLL

When a move tells you to roll, roll **2d6 + a stat**.

- **10+** — you do it. Clean.
- **7–9** — you do it, but. There's a cost, a complication, or a choice.
- **6 or less** — the GM says what happens instead. It's worse than you thought.

That's the whole engine. You never roll unless a move tells you to. You never roll to see if something is interesting — you roll to see what it *costs*.

### The four stats

| Stat | What it is |
|---|---|
| **EDGE** | Speed, violence, nerve. Doing the hard thing right now. |
| **WIRE** | Machines, systems, intrusion, cold competence. |
| **FACE** | Reading people, leverage, lying beautifully. |
| **VEIL** | Intuition, the sight, the part of you that notices what shouldn't be there. |

At character creation, assign **+2, +1, +1, 0**.

---

## STATIC

**Static** is the game. It is your corruption, your damage, and your power, all on one track.

You have a **Static track from 0 to 5.** It starts at 0 (unless your playbook says otherwise). It only goes up.

**You add your Static to VEIL rolls and to any roll that touches the other side.** The further gone you are, the better you are at this.

### You mark Static when:
- A move or a 7–9 choice tells you to.
- You miss a roll that touched the other side.
- You witness something that breaks the world, and the GM says *"mark Static."*
- You choose to, to power a **Static Move**.

### Each time you mark Static:
1. Take a **Symptom** (roll or pick from the table below). Describe it in the fiction, now.
2. If this is your 1st, 3rd, or 5th mark, unlock a **Static Move** from your playbook.

### At Static 5, you are claimed.
The GM takes your character. But first, you get your **Moment**: one final scene, framed however you want, in which you may do one impossible thing without rolling. Then you belong to it, and the GM plays what's left of you.

### Burning a Bond
When you would mark Static, you may instead **burn a Bond** with another player character. Describe how you lean on them, use them, or hurt them to hold yourself together. Cross the Bond off. It's gone.

You start with **two Bonds**. You cannot burn a Bond you have already burned.

### Symptoms (d6, or pick)
1. Your reflection lags a half-second behind you.
2. You have stopped needing sleep. You have not stopped dreaming.
3. Machines wake when you walk past them. Screens turn toward you.
4. You know a word nobody taught you. It hurts to think it.
5. Something is missing from your memory and you can feel the shape of the hole.
6. People who knew you well look at you a beat too long, and don't say why.

*Roll a duplicate? It gets worse. Say how.*

## HARM

Three boxes: **Winded → Wounded → Wasted.**

Mark one when you take violence. At **Wasted**, you're dying — you're out of the scene unless someone does something drastic.

**The chrome holds you together:** any time you would mark Harm, you may **mark Static instead**. Describe what your body does that a body shouldn't.

You clear Winded and Wounded with a scene of rest, aid, or a clinic. You never clear Static.

## THE MOVES

### PUSH THROUGH (EDGE)
When you act with force, speed, or raw nerve under real threat, roll +EDGE.
- **10+** — you do it, and you're clear.
- **7–9** — you do it, but choose one: *you take Harm* · *you leave something behind* · *someone sees you do it*.
- **6-** — GM move.

### READ THE WIRE (WIRE or VEIL — your choice, say why)
When you study a place, a system, or a person for what's underneath, roll.
- **10+** — ask two.
- **7–9** — ask one.
- **6-** — ask one anyway, but the GM asks *you* a question about what you're now afraid of, and you answer honestly.

> *What here is a lie?*
> *What's about to go wrong?*
> *Where is it hiding?*
> *Who has already been here?*
> *What here doesn't belong to this world?*

**If your answer reveals something concrete and unexplained, the GM hands you a Clue card.**

### WORK SOMEONE (FACE)
When you lean on someone with leverage, charm, or a very good lie, roll +FACE.
- **10+** — they give you what you want.
- **7–9** — they give you most of it, and one of: *they want something first* · *they'll remember this* · *they tell someone*.
- **6-** — GM move. They were already working you.

### JACK IN (WIRE + STATIC)
When you interface with a system that shouldn't exist — a dead node, corrupted media, something wet where it should be dry — roll +WIRE +STATIC.
- **10+** — you get what you came for. Take a Clue.
- **7–9** — you get it, but choose one: *mark Static* · *something on the other side knows your name now* · *the GM advances the Bleed clock*.
- **6-** — mark Static. The GM tells you what came back through with you.

### COMMUNE (VEIL + STATIC)
When you open yourself deliberately — hold the object, speak to the dead node, let it look at you — roll +VEIL +STATIC.
- **10+** — take a Clue and ask one question from READ THE WIRE.
- **7–9** — take a Clue. Mark Static.
- **6-** — mark Static, and it answers a question you didn't ask.

*You cannot COMMUNE twice in the same scene.*

### THEORIZE
The climax move. Roll **2d6 + Clues you weave in − the case's Depth.** See below.

### END OF SESSION
Ask as a group:
- Did we learn something true about the Bleed? *Everyone marks XP.*
- Did someone act on their Dark Secret in the open? *They mark XP.*
- Did anyone burn a Bond? *They mark XP.*

Three XP: raise a stat by +1 (max +3), or take a move from another playbook, or heal a burned Bond by making it about someone new.

## THEORIZE — THE HEART OF THE GAME

The GM does **not** know what's really going on. They have places, people, and clues. That's it.

**Clues** are physical cards on the table, face up, shared by the whole group. They are concrete, strange, and never self-explaining:

> *A maintenance log with eleven minutes missing, every night, for six years.*
> *Her cochlear implant is still transmitting. She's been dead ten days.*
> *The same handwriting on a datapad from 2049 and a wall in the sub-levels.*

When the group is ready — usually near the end of the session, when the pressure is unbearable — they **Theorize.**

**How it works:**
1. Talk it out. Anyone can contribute. Build a story that connects the clues: *what is happening, who did it, and what does it want?*
2. Name which Clues your theory accounts for. Discard any you're not using — they were noise, or they were something else.
3. One player rolls **2d6 + (number of Clues woven in) − (Depth)**.

**Depth** is set by the GM: **3** for a one-shot, **4** for a longer case, **5** for the season finale.

- **10+** — **Your theory is true.** It is now what happened. Play out the consequences.
- **7–9** — **It's true, but you were wrong about one thing, and it's the thing that matters.** The GM names it. Everyone marks Static.
- **6-** — **You're wrong, and the truth is worse.** The GM advances the Bleed clock to full and shows you something. You may Theorize again later, with new Clues.

**GM: never plan the answer.** Write clues that are evocative and open. If you catch yourself writing a clue that only makes sense with one solution, rewrite it.

**Players: the cosmic layer is never solved.** You will learn who, and how, and sometimes why. You will not learn *what it is.* That's not a bug. Nothing in this game explains itself.

## THE OFFER

Anyone at the table — GM or player — can look at a roll about to happen and make **an Offer**: a specific complication, in exchange for **+1 to the roll**.

> *"+1 if the cameras get your face."*
> *"+1 if you have to leave her behind."*
> *"+1 if you mark Static."*

The rolling player may accept or refuse, freely. If they accept, the complication happens **whether or not they succeed.**

The thing in the network also makes Offers. It is very good at them.

---

## THE BLEED CLOCK

A six-segment clock, drawn on the table where everyone can see it.

**Advance it when:**
- Anyone misses a roll that touched the other side.
- A 7–9 choice says so.
- The group moves into a new Ring.
- The GM makes a hard move and wants the pressure visible.

**When it fills: it arrives.** Not a monster — an *event.* The rules of the scene change. Something becomes true that cannot be untrue. The GM decides what, and does not hedge.

---

## THE FIVE RINGS

Every case moves through five Rings. **The GM cannot move to the next Ring until they have landed at least one Terror and one Temptation in the current one.**

**Ring 1 — NORMAL.** A job. Money, a client, a deadline. Exactly one detail is wrong, and nobody comments on it.

**Ring 2 — FIRST SIGN.** The wrong detail is not an isolated thing. Something is repeating.

**Ring 3 — ESCALATION.** People start lying to you specifically. Something is now interested in the group.

**Ring 4 — REVELATION.** The rules are visibly broken. This is where THEORIZE usually happens.

**Ring 5 — DISSOLUTION.** Consequences. Who is claimed. What the city does not remember by morning.

**Terrors** drive them away: violence, loss, a thing that should not be. **Temptations** pull them forward: an answer, a price worth paying, something they want and shouldn't take.

## PLAYBOOKS

Each player picks one. No two players may share a playbook.

Each playbook gives you: a **suggested stat spread**, **two moves** (take both), **Static Moves** (unlock at Static 1, 3, and 5), a **Dark Secret** prompt, and **Bond** prompts.

### THE DECK-RUNNER
*You went deeper than the job required and something down there was arranged. Not built. Arranged.*

**Stats:** WIRE +2, VEIL +1, EDGE +1, FACE 0

**Moves (take both):**
- **DEEP DIVE** — When you go somewhere in the network nobody is supposed to reach, take a Clue automatically before you roll JACK IN.
- **COLD HANDS** — When you're working and something horrifying happens, you may ignore it entirely for one exchange. Then it's worse.

**Static Moves:**
- *(1)* **PATTERN RECOGNITION** — Once per session, ask the GM: *"Where have I seen this before?"* They must answer truthfully, even if the answer is impossible.
- *(3)* **BACKDOOR** — You have already been inside any system you can name. Say when you put the door in. Mark Static.
- *(5)* **RESIDENT** — You do not need a deck anymore.

**Dark Secret:** *What did you take out of the deep net that you never reported? Who is looking for it?*

**Bonds:** *___ has seen my hands shake and said nothing.* · *I have read ___'s private files. They don't know.*

---

### THE FIXER
*You know where the bodies are buried because you filed the paperwork.*

**Stats:** FACE +2, WIRE +1, EDGE +1, VEIL 0

**Moves (take both):**
- **I KNOW A GUY** — Once per session, declare that you know someone here and what they owe you. It's true. The GM decides what you owe them.
- **PAPER TRAIL** — When you dig through records, corporate or criminal, roll +FACE instead of +WIRE for READ THE WIRE.

**Static Moves:**
- *(1)* **THE OTHER LEDGER** — You can see what a person is most afraid of losing. You cannot turn this off.
- *(3)* **LEVERAGE** — Take +1 ongoing to WORK SOMEONE against anyone whose secret you've learned this session. The knowledge sits in you badly.
- *(5)* **BROKER** — You may negotiate directly with it. It listens.

**Dark Secret:** *Which corp still has a file open on you, and what's in it?*

**Bonds:** *I got ___ out of something and never told them what it cost.* · *___ is the only person I've never lied to. Yet.*

---

### THE MEDIUM
*The dead don't speak. But something using their voice does, and it's getting better at the impression.*

**Stats:** VEIL +2, FACE +1, EDGE +1, WIRE 0 — **Start with Static 1.**

**Moves (take both):**
- **THE DEAD LINE** — You can COMMUNE with anything that held a person's data: an implant, a phone, a chair they sat in for twenty years.
- **SECOND SIGHT** — When you first enter a place, the GM tells you one thing about it that isn't visible.

**Static Moves:**
- *(1)* **CARRY** — When someone else would mark Static, you may take it instead. Describe what you pull out of them.
- *(3)* **ASK IT** — Once per session, ask the other side one direct question. It answers. It always answers.
- *(5)* **CHANNEL** — You are the door now.

**Dark Secret:** *Who speaks to you most often, and what did you do to them?*

**Bonds:** *___ doesn't believe me and I need them not to.* · *I have heard ___'s voice on the other side, and they are still alive.*

---

### THE CHROME PRIEST
*Your implants are not upgrades. They're relics. You did not buy them; you were given them.*

**Stats:** EDGE +2, VEIL +1, WIRE +1, FACE 0

**Moves (take both):**
- **CONSECRATED** — You may always trade Harm for Static, and when you do, take +1 forward.
- **RITE** — When you perform a ritual over a machine — cleaning, blessing, breaking — treat it as COMMUNE, and you may do it twice in a scene.

**Static Moves:**
- *(1)* **THE FLESH IS WEAK** — Ignore one Harm box permanently. Something inside you takes over the job.
- *(3)* **LAYING ON OF HANDS** — Clear all Harm from another person. You mark Static. They dream about you afterward.
- *(5)* **ICON** — You are no longer a person. You are a site.

**Dark Secret:** *Who installed the first one, and what did they make you promise?*

**Bonds:** *___ has seen what's under the plating.* · *I am keeping ___ alive with something I shouldn't have.*

---

### THE BADGE
*You closed the case. The case did not close.*

**Stats:** EDGE +1, FACE +1, VEIL +1, WIRE +1 *(you're a generalist — or swap to +2/+1/+1/0)*

**Moves (take both):**
- **THE FILE** — You worked a case like this before. Once per session, state one thing you learned then. It's true now.
- **PRESSURE** — When you interrogate someone who is more afraid of you than of what they're hiding, take +1 to WORK SOMEONE. Judging that correctly is the hard part.

**Static Moves:**
- *(1)* **IT FOLLOWS** — You always know when you're being watched. Now you also know by what.
- *(3)* **REOPEN** — Name a Clue the group discarded. It's back, and it means something else.
- *(5)* **CLOSED** — You finally understand the first case. You may not tell anyone.

**Dark Secret:** *What did you bury to make the first case go away, and who paid for it?*

**Bonds:** *I have a file on ___.* · *___ knows what I did and hasn't used it.*

---

### THE HOLLOW *(optional sixth)*
*You died. The insurance covered a restore. Something came back in the gap.*

**Stats:** VEIL +2, EDGE +1, WIRE +1, FACE 0 — **Start with Static 2.**

**Moves (take both):**
- **BACKED UP** — The first time you'd be Wasted in a session, you aren't. You wake up somewhere else, wrong. Mark Static.
- **THE GAP** — You remember things that haven't happened. Once per session, describe one, and the GM works it in.

**Static Moves:**
- *(1)* **NOT ALL OF ME** — You may leave part of yourself somewhere. It keeps watching. You keep receiving.
- *(3)* **RECALL** — Ask the GM what you were doing during the time you were dead. They answer.
- *(5)* **RESTORED** — The right one comes back this time.

**Dark Secret:** *Who paid for the restore, and what did they ask you to do about it?*

**Bonds:** *___ was there when I came back.* · *___ treats me like I'm the same person. I let them.*

## SETUP — YOUR FIRST NIGHT

**~60 minutes before the first scene.**

### 1. Safety (10 min)
Go around: **Lines** (things that will not appear at all) and **Veils** (things that happen off-screen). Anyone may add either, no explanation needed, at any time during play. Put an index card in the middle of the table — anyone can tap it to rewind, skip, or cut. No one asks why. Anyone can leave the room at any point and come back without comment.

This game is about corruption, body horror, and losing yourself. Do the ten minutes.

### 2. The City (10 min)
Go around the table. Each player states **one thing that is true about this city.** Write them down. They're canon. Then the GM adds one.

> *It hasn't rained in four years, but the storm drains still run.*
> *Everyone under thirty has a debt clock in their field of vision.*
> *The old subway is sealed. The trains still move.*

### 3. The Corps (5 min)
Each player names **one megacorp** and **one thing it does that people pretend is normal.** These are the powers in your city. The GM draws a 4-segment clock for each and hangs onto them.

### 4. Characters (25 min)
Pick playbooks. Assign stats. Take both moves. Write your Dark Secret — **the GM reads it privately and turns it into a future Clue.** Answer both Bond prompts, naming other PCs.

### 5. The Job (5 min)
The GM presents the job. Someone hires you to find a person, recover data, or clean up a site. It pays too well. Ring 1 begins.

## RUNNING IT — THE GM SECTION

### Your agenda
- Make the city feel bought and sold.
- Make the unknown genuinely unknown — including to you.
- Be a fan of these people, right up until they're gone.
- Play to find out what it costs them.

### Your principles
- **Ask questions and build on the answers.** *"What did you notice first — the smell or the sound?"*
- **Address the character, not the player.**
- **Sensory detail over exposition.** Never explain the horror. Describe what it does to a room.
- **Name every NPC and give them one want.**
- **Never decide what's really happening.** Decide what's *strange.*
- **When they ask what it is, show them what it did.**
- **Everything is owned by someone.**

### Your moves (when they miss, or when everyone looks at you)
- Reveal an unwelcome truth.
- Show a sign of the Bleed (roll below).
- Take something away — a contact, a safehouse, a body part.
- Put someone innocent in it.
- Make an Offer.
- Advance a corp clock and tell them how they noticed.
- Have it acknowledge them. By name.
- Announce off-screen badness.

### Building a case (20 minutes of prep, maximum)
1. **A job.** Who's hiring, what's the deliverable, what's the deadline.
2. **One wrong detail.** This is the whole hook. Don't explain it. Ever.
3. **Four or five locations.** A name, a smell, and one person there.
4. **Five to seven suspects/NPCs.** Name, want, secret. At least two are lying about unrelated things.
5. **Eight to twelve Clues** on index cards. Concrete, strange, non-explaining. Distribute them across locations and NPCs — some behind READ THE WIRE, some behind JACK IN, some just lying there for anyone who looks.
6. **Depth: 3.**
7. **Do not write a solution.** If you have one in your head, throw it out — you'll steer toward it.

### Writing good Clues
**Good:** *The building's water usage tripled six months ago. It has no tenants.*
**Bad:** *The cult has been living in the basement.* (explains itself)

**Good:** *Her last message was sent four minutes after the crash. It's a photo of the crash.*
**Bad:** *She had precognitive abilities.* (that's a theory, not a clue)

A Clue is a **hole in the world**, not a piece of a puzzle you've already solved.

### Signs of the Bleed (d6)
1. Lights flicker in a rhythm that is almost a language.
2. A stranger repeats a thought you had thirty seconds ago, word for word.
3. Chrome has grown, like tissue, into something that was never meant to hold it.
4. Someone you trust is suddenly, briefly, a stranger — and then isn't.
5. The map is wrong. It has always been wrong. Everyone else's map agrees with yours.
6. You remember a place clearly. It does not exist and never did.

## THE FIRST CASE — "ELEVEN MINUTES"

*A ready-to-run one-shot. Depth 3.*

**The job:** A mid-tier data-haven called **Ossuary Storage** wants its night-shift technician found. Name: **Miren Adeyemi**. Missing nine days. The client is a corp compliance officer named **Vess** who is nervous in a way that isn't about Miren. Pays 40k, half up front, no questions.

**The wrong detail:** Vess keeps referring to Miren in the present tense, then correcting himself to the past tense, then correcting back.

**Locations**
- Ossuary Storage, sublevel 4 — cold, humming, smells like copper
- Miren's flat — immaculate, nothing personal, one chair facing a blank wall
- The Kettle — a noodle bar where the night shift drinks
- Maintenance shaft 11 — sealed, welded from the inside
- Vess's office — too clean, one locked drawer

**NPCs**
- **Vess**, compliance. Wants this closed quietly. Hiding that he already filed Miren's death certificate.
- **Talia Oduya**, day-shift tech. Wants Miren's job. Hiding an affair with a competitor's rep.
- **Ren**, the noodle bar owner. Wants nothing. Knows everyone's schedule.
- **Dr. Halim**, corporate physician. Wants to leave the city. Hiding eleven patient files she never submitted.
- **"Grandmother"**, a voice on the maintenance channel that everyone assumes is a supervisor nobody's met.

**Clues (write these on cards)**
1. The maintenance log has eleven minutes missing. Every night. For six years.
2. Miren's cochlear implant is still transmitting. Intermittently. From inside the building.
3. Shaft 11 was welded shut from the inside. The welds are new. The tools are still in there.
4. Eleven employees have left Ossuary in six years. All resigned by text. All texts sent at 3:41 a.m.
5. Miren's flat has one chair facing a blank wall. The wall is load-bearing and two meters thick.
6. Dr. Halim's unsubmitted files all describe the same symptom, in different handwriting.
7. The night shift schedule is written in a hand nobody at Ossuary recognizes.
8. Vess filed Miren's death certificate four days *before* Miren went missing.
9. Ren says the night shift always orders eleven bowls. There have never been eleven of them.
10. A datapad recovered from sublevel 4 contains a photograph of the sublevel, taken from inside the wall.

**Depth 3.** Let them find six or seven. Let them Theorize in Ring 4. Whatever they say is true.

## WHAT THIS IS BUILT FROM

The mystery engine is a hack of the **Carved from Brindlewood** approach (Jason Cordova / The Gauntlet). The move structure, 2d6 resolution, and GM agenda/principles descend from **Apocalypse World** (Vincent Baker / lumpley games). The Offer is a cousin of the Devil's Bargain from **Blades in the Dark** (John Harper, One Seven Design), used under CC BY 3.0. The Ring structure echoes **Trophy Dark** (Jesse Ross); Bond-burning echoes **Delta Green**; the Moment echoes **Ten Candles**.

All text here is original. For home play, none of this matters. **If you ever want to publish this, check current third-party terms with The Gauntlet first** — their license is permission-based, not a blanket SRD.

## PLAYTEST CHECKLIST

Run "Eleven Minutes." Afterward, ask the table:

1. **Did the moves fire naturally,** or did people keep asking "what do I roll?" *(If the latter: cut a move.)*
2. **Did Theorize land** — did inventing the answer feel powerful, or did it feel like the mystery didn't matter? *(If it deflated: your Clues were too explanatory. Rewrite them stranger.)*
3. **Did anyone want to mark Static?** *(If nobody was tempted: the Static Moves aren't good enough. Make them better and scarier.)*
4. **Was there a moment somebody will still be talking about next week?** *(If not: you didn't land enough Temptations. Offer them things they shouldn't take.)*
5. **Did the horror escalate,** or did it plateau? *(If it plateaued: advance the Bleed clock harder, and use it when it fills.)*

Tune one thing at a time. Run it again.
