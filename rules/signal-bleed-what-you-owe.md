# WHAT YOU OWE
### A campaign module for SIGNAL BLEED
*The block, the list, and the boats. Use with The Ossuary Sequence or any season.*

---

## WHY THIS EXISTS

The season can be won. Stopping the Stand means getting the people off the Hours — evacuation, dispersal, a hundred and forty years of invented culture scattered across a continent that has never heard of it. The crew makes that call themselves, for everybody, and they don't find out it's the price until the end.

For that ending to land, the table has to know specific people. Not *the community* — **Auntie Sesi at number nineteen, who you owe for the generator.** Abstractions don't hurt. Names do.

So this module does three things:

1. Gives the crew **a block they all grew up on**, seeded at character creation.
2. Turns every interlude into **one more name on a visible list**, with what you owe them.
3. Makes that list **the evacuation manifest** in the finale — and gives you the arithmetic for how many berths there are.

The players build the invoice themselves, in their own handwriting, across the whole campaign. They just don't know what it's for.

---

## PART ONE — PROVIDENT ROW

**The crew grew up on the same block.** Not friends necessarily — neighbours, which is stronger and more complicated.

**Provident Row**, Low Town. Two terraces facing each other across a street too narrow for vehicles, running down toward the harbour wall the neighbourhood built itself. Built 1911 by the Calder-Vaughn Provident Society as cannery housing and never once maintained by them since. Concrete, salt-eaten, patched by residents with whatever came off a ship. Washing lines across the gap. Everyone's grandparents were assigned here. Nobody chose it and nobody leaves.

The doors are numbered 1 to 44.

**There is no number 11.**

There is no gap in the terrace, no bricked-up doorway, no space where a house should be. Number 10 and number 12 share a wall. The council register lists 43 units. Count the doors and you get 43. The 1911 deed says 44, and the Provident Society was dissolved in 1962, and nobody has ever been able to explain it.

Everyone on the Row has known this since they were a child. It's a joke. You tell it to people who move in.

> **GM: never resolve this.** It's not a clue and it doesn't pay off. It sits in the setting being wrong, forever, exactly like the eleven minutes and the eleven islands and the eleventh bowl. The Row has always had the shape of the thing in it. That's all.

### Seeding the Row at character creation

After playbooks, before the first job. Twenty minutes and it pays for the whole campaign.

**Go around the table twice.**

**First pass — each player names a household.** A door number, who lives behind it, and one concrete thing about them. Not plot hooks. Furniture.

> *"Number 19, Auntie Sesi. She's not anyone's auntie. She's had the same generator running since before I was born and she'll fix yours for nothing if you sit and listen to her first."*

**Second pass — each player says what their character owes that household.** Not a favour owed to *them* — something *your character* owes. Start the campaign already in debt.

> *"She kept the lights on the year my mother was sick and never once mentioned it after."*

**Then the GM adds two more households** — one the crew doesn't like, one nobody knows well. The Row isn't sentimental. Somebody on it is a nuisance and somebody on it is a stranger.

That's six to eight names before the first scene. **Write every one on the list.**

## PART TWO — THE LIST

Keep it where everyone can see it. A shared page, a whiteboard, the app. Two columns:

| Name | What you owe them |
|---|---|
| Auntie Sesi, no. 19 | Kept the lights on the year my mother was sick |
| Ren, the Kettle | Fed the whole crew for a month on credit and never sent a bill |
| Tavi Ruiz, no. 3 | I didn't say anything when they took his brother |

**Rules of the list:**

- **It only grows.** Nothing is ever removed. Like Static.
- **Every interlude scene adds exactly one line.** No exceptions, no skipping.
- **Anyone can add to it during play**, not just in interludes, if a scene earns it — but the GM says yes or no.
- **The debt is always specific and always past tense.** Not "she might help us." *"She already did, and I never paid it back."*
- **Nobody explains what the list is for.** Not the GM, not the sheets, not the app. It's just the list. It sits there getting longer.

When a player asks what it's for, the honest answer is: *"It's what you owe."* That's true. It's also, eventually, the manifest.

## PART THREE — THE REVISED INTERLUDE

Replaces the interlude in The Ossuary Sequence. Same five options; each now **must** produce a name and a debt.

**Each player takes one scene.** Frame it, say who's there, then choose:

**RECOVER** — Clear all Harm. *Who patched you up, and what do you owe them for it?*

**DIG** — Ask the GM one Read the Wire question, no roll. The answer is true and someone noticed you asking. *Who covered for you?*

**REPAIR** — Sit with someone whose Bond you burned. Play it honestly. If it's honest, rewrite that Bond and unburn it. *Once per interlude.* Add the person who made the room for that conversation.

**FEED IT** — You went looking. Mark Static, take the Static Move it unlocks. *Who noticed you were different afterward, and said nothing?*

**GO HOME** — A normal evening. The GM narrates one small specific thing that goes wrong with it. Take +1 forward. *Who was there, and what did they do for you?*

Then, as a table: read the whole list aloud, top to bottom, before the next case starts. Every time. It takes ninety seconds and by the fourth interlude it will have started to feel like something.

## PART FOUR — THE BERTHS

**The finale, Ring 5.** They've theorized. They understand what's coming and what stops it. The Stand can be closed, and closing it means the Hours are done — the chain has to be empty before it opens, and there is a finite amount of hull leaving the harbour.

**Read the list aloud. Then tell them how many berths there are.**

```
BERTHS =  6
       + (2 × unburned Bonds across the whole crew)
       − (every task the Watch crossed off the list)
       + (final Theorize:  10+ → +4  ·  7–9 → +2  ·  6− → 0)
```

Count it in front of them. Slowly.

**This is where the campaign comes due.** Every Bond a character burned to avoid a Static mark — every time someone leaned on a friend to hold themselves together — cost two people a place on a boat. Nobody said so at the time. Nobody lied, either; the rule was always *burning a bond spends a person.* They just found out the exchange rate at the end.

Same for the Watch's **You Hold the Line**. Every task crossed off was something the neighbourhood needed that didn't get done. It bought a character one clean night. It costs a berth now.

**Then the crew decides. Alone.**

They read names off their own list and cross out the ones who aren't going. Out loud, one at a time, with reasons. The GM says nothing, offers nothing, and does not help.

### Say this part plainly

**The crew deciding alone, for everyone, is the exact thing this setting is about.** A hundred and forty years of people with clipboards deciding what happens to the Hours and the people on them: the Provident Society, the phosphate contracts, the naval station, the registry, the Sounding Company. If your crew does it too, they're the last name on that list.

That's not a misfire. That's the ending — *if the game knows.*

So **put the Tide Watch in the room.** Have someone say *you don't get to do this to us.* Let them be completely right. Let the crew do it anyway, because the alternative is that nobody leaves at all.

If a player has the Watch playbook, they are in this scene and they have to raise their hand. Don't spare them. It's their ledger.

**Nobody is a villain here.** The people left behind aren't punished and they don't die on screen. They stay. They're on the Row when it closes, and nobody knows what that means, and that's worse than knowing.

---

## PART FIVE — HOW TO END IT

Don't end on the boats. Boats are an image of departure and this isn't about leaving, it's about what's left.

**End on Provident Row.** Morning, after. The washing lines. The doors, 1 to 44, with no number 11. Some doors open, some not.

Go round the table and ask each player one question:

> **"What did you leave behind in the house, and who was it for?"**

Let them answer. Don't comment. Don't summarise.

Then read the crossed-out names once, and close the book.

## GM CHECKLIST

- ☐ Run the Row seeding at character creation. Don't skip it to get to the job faster.
- ☐ Keep the list visible every session.
- ☐ Read it aloud at every interlude. Every single one.
- ☐ Never explain what it's for.
- ☐ Never explain number 11.
- ☐ When a Bond burns, say nothing. Just make sure it's written down.
- ☐ In the finale, count the berths in front of them, slowly.
- ☐ Put the Watch in the room.
- ☐ Don't gloat. This is grief, not punishment.
