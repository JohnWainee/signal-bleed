# WHAT YOU OWE
## Hawaiʻi household and obligation module

Use with [Hawaiʻi — Emergency Republic](hawaii-setting-bible.md). This adaptation retains personal stakes without the former predetermined island-evacuation ending.

## The network

Each player names one fictional household or person in a real place and one concrete thing they already owe them. The crew can be connected through work, family, neighbors or the emergency response; a shared childhood is optional. The GM adds two people, including someone the crew does not know well. Fictional people/premises are not assertions about real residents.

| Person | What you owe |
|---|---|
| A neighbor in Kalihi | Kept the generator running while you missed a shift |
| Ren, owner of the fictional Kettle canteen in Chinatown | Fed the crew on credit |
| A dispatch colleague in the fictional harbor response team | Challenged a forecast so you could check its source |

## The list

Record person, obligation, origin scene and status. Keep the history when a debt is met, disputed or changes; do not erase the person. Each interlude introduces or develops a specific connection. Shared display contains only details the group has agreed to reveal; private notes remain private.

## Interlude moves

Preserve the existing five choices from The Ossuary Sequence:
- **Recover:** clear Harm; name who helped.
- **Dig:** one Read the Wire question, with someone noticing.
- **Repair:** an honest Bond repair scene, once per interlude.
- **Feed It:** mark Static and take the move it unlocks.
- **Go Home:** an ordinary evening with a small complication; take +1 forward.

Connect the scene to a named person and a specific obligation. Narrative obligations are not extra mechanical Bonds or automatic penalties.

## A finale earned through play

Use the final theory, established evidence and scenario date to determine what choices are available. A successful intervention may have displaced a cost; identify the affected people rather than declaring a universal cosmic exchange rate.

Do not presume that saving Hawaiʻi requires depopulating it. Local evacuation, testimony, access negotiation or refusal of a program can arise from play. If a scene uses a finite capacity, establish that capacity and its costs openly before choices. The former hidden “burned Bonds cost boat seats” ending does not apply by default.

The Watch's task-crossing move retains its immediate fictional cost: something needed is not done. It does not secretly remove a later evacuation seat. A capacity minigame would require a separately agreed scenario rule.

End on a concrete return to ordinary life: a missed shift covered, a door opened, a statement corrected, a call answered. Ask what the character does next and what remains owed.
