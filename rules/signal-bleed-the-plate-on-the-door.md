# THE PLATE ON THE DOOR
### SIGNAL BLEED — campaign spine
*Everything decided across six rounds, in one place. Use with the core rules, The Hours setting bible, and WHAT YOU OWE.*

---

## THE ARGUMENT

Every extraction in the history of the Hours arrived as help.

The Provident Society built housing. The cannery gave people work. The registry gave a flag to anyone who asked, in forty minutes, no questions. All of it was offered kindly, all of it worked exactly as promised, and all of it took a hundred and forty years to finish collecting.

The thing in the deep water southeast of Compline is polite, generous, and never explains itself, because **that is what this place taught it to be.** It learned its manner from the paperwork.

This is why the game is winnable and why winning is unbearable. The Stand can be stopped. Stopping it means emptying the chain — evacuation, dispersal, a hundred and forty years of invented culture scattered across a continent that has never heard of it. And the people who make that call are a small licensed firm with a plate on the door, deciding on behalf of everyone what the record will say happened.

Which is their job. It always was. This time it's just big enough to see.

**The register is melancholy, not anger.** Nobody in this story is a villain. That's the problem.

---

## THE FIRM

**HALLORAN & VEY — Marine Casualty & Loss Adjustment.**
Third floor above a chandlery on the Crown. One window. A brass plate by the street door that somebody polishes without being paid to.

The 1961 registry ordinance required two named principals. When Halloran set up, he needed a second name, so he invented one. **There was never a Vey.** Halloran himself died nine years ago and the plate passed to you.

So the crew trades under two names that aren't there — one dead, one imaginary — in a city that sells flags to ships that aren't there, investigating an hour that isn't there. Nobody has ever found this strange. It is completely legal.

**What the firm actually does:** determines what happened. Wrecks, losses, disappearances, insurance findings, compliance work nobody senior wants. The work product is a **finding** — a document that says, officially, what occurred. In Port Vespers that document *is* the truth, because Port Vespers has no other kind.

### Otty

**Ottoline Sabban.** Sixty-one. Thirty-four years at Halloran & Vey.

She started under Halloran when she was twenty-seven. She knows where every file is, including the ones from before the crew existed. The year he died, she is the sole reason the plate was renewed on time, because she did the paperwork without being asked and without mentioning it.

The crew inherited a name. **Otty inherited the work.** She is the actual continuity of the firm.

She answers the phone *"Halloran and Vey"* in a way that makes it sound like both of them are just down the hall.

She goes on the list at character creation, before anyone else. **The crew have never once asked her what she wants.** When the berths get counted, she will be standing right there.

## THE LICENCE

The plate is **conditional, and already has been for years.** It gets renewed annually by the Deputy Registrar of Marine Licences, and it gets renewed because she chooses to renew it.

**Mrs. Ansel.** She has stamped your renewal eleven years running. She is unfailingly pleasant, asks after your mother by name, and **has never once asked you for anything** — which is considerably worse than if she had.

She is not Long Count. She is an ordinary, decent civil servant doing her job kindly. The resemblance is the entire point, and the game should never resolve it.

### The licence in play

The licence is the brake on violence, and it is the only one.

> **Violence is always available and always a mistake.** It never closes a case. It can only make the next scene harder.

- When the crew uses force to solve something, **the GM advances the Bleed** and someone on the list is worse off for it.
- Serious violence gets the plate **looked at**. Looked at means an inspection, a hearing, a conditional renewal, a favour called in.
- **Losing the plate means losing access.** No licence, no findings, no doors, no records, no reason for anyone to talk to you.

The punishment for violence is not death. It is **becoming useless to the people you owe.** In a game about debts you can no longer pay, that is the worst available outcome, and it is entirely recoverable-looking right up until it isn't.

## THE STAND

**Never define what it is. Define how it behaves.**

> Unfailingly helpful. Never explains itself. Always gives you slightly more than you asked for.

**The name is a tide term.** The *stand* is the interval when the water is neither rising nor falling — an ordinary thing every fisherman says without thinking. *It's only the stand. It'll turn.* A hundred and forty years of people have said that to each other and it has never once been a lie, and it is not a lie now, and that is not the same as it being true.

**Someone taken all the way has gone *off soundings*** — water too deep to measure. Nobody says it unkindly.

That's the whole specification. Hold that line and it will stay unknowable across an entire campaign, which is the only way cosmic horror survives contact with a table that invents its own answers.

### The help always works

This is the load-bearing rule and the hardest one to hold.

When the Stand helps, **the help is real.** Concrete, correct, generous, and it solves the problem. No riddles, no monkey's paw, no ironic reversal. It does exactly what it offered to do.

The horror is not that it lies. **The horror is that it doesn't, and you take the help anyway, and so did everyone before you.**

If you make its help a trap, you have written a different and much smaller game. Refusing the Stand must be a genuine sacrifice, made for reasons the character cannot fully articulate, against their own immediate interest. That's the only version where saying no means anything.

### The Long Count is a symptom

Not a cult. Not a faction. **A condition.**

There is no leadership to cut off, no meeting to infiltrate, no headquarters to raid. You cannot fight it, which reinforces everything above about violence.

**The diagnostic criterion is the manner.** Someone has started being helpful in that particular way — obliging, unexplaining, generous past the point of the request. That's it. That's how you know.

Anyone can develop it. NPCs the crew loves. NPCs the crew relies on. **Player characters at high Static.**

## NEW MOVES

### SAY IT YOURSELF
*At Static 3 or higher.*

> When you catch your character having been helpful in a way you can't account for, **name it out loud at the table.** Mark XP.
>
> If you don't say it and the GM notices first, **mark Static instead.**

Confession is protective. Silence compounds. This is the only move in the game that rewards a player for turning on their own character, and by Static 4 the rest of the table will be watching for it before the player is.

### THE WATER
*GM move, once per session, at the top.*

Open every case with **one sentence about the water.** Same shape every time, one increment worse.

> *"The third step down from the Row is under now. Nobody's mentioned it."*
> *"The chandlery's put its stock on pallets. It didn't used to need to."*
> *"They've stopped repainting the lower units on the Strand."*

Weeks pass between cases. This is the Aperture made diegetic — the players will start bracing for the sentence before you say it.

### PLAYING WHAT YOU LOST
*When your character is claimed at Static 5.*

> You lose the character. **You keep the voice.**
>
> Roll up someone new. When the GM decides your claimed character appears, **you play them.** You decide what help they offer.
>
> The help has to be real. Concrete, correct, generous. No riddles.

You will sit at the table watching your friends take advice from you that they know they shouldn't take and cannot afford to refuse. It is the best seat at the table and the worst one.

---

## TIME

**Weeks between cases.** Long enough that the water is measurably higher, short enough that nobody has moved on.

This buys you something important: **people can just leave.** Between cases, a name on the list emigrates to the continent, or takes a berth on a container run, or dies of something entirely ordinary — and the crew finds out late, secondhand, from someone else.

Losing someone to nothing in particular is sadder than losing them to the Stand. Do it at least twice before the finale.

---

## THE PRICE

Full procedure in **WHAT YOU OWE**. In summary:

- The crew grew up on **Provident Row**, Low Town. Doors 1 to 44. There is no number 11, there never was, and nobody has ever explained it. *Never resolve this.*
- Every interlude scene adds one line to a **visible list**: a name, and what you owe them. It only grows.
- In the finale, that list is **the evacuation manifest**.

```
BERTHS =  6
       + (2 × unburned Bonds across the crew)
       − (every task the Watch crossed off)
       + (final Theorize:  10+ → +4  ·  7–9 → +2  ·  6− → 0)
```

Every Bond burned to dodge a Static mark cost two people a place on a boat. Nobody lied about it — the rule always said burning a Bond spends a person. They simply learn the exchange rate at the end, holding a list they wrote themselves.

**The crew decides alone, out loud, with the Tide Watch in the room saying *you don't get to do this to us*** — and being completely right, and being overruled, because the alternative is that nobody leaves at all.

## SESSION ONE CHECKLIST

- ☐ Safety: Lines & Veils, the card on the table, the open door. Ten minutes. Do it.
- ☐ Establish four things that are true about the Hours (go round the table).
- ☐ **Seed Provident Row** — each player names a household and what they already owe it. GM adds two. *(~8 names before the first scene.)*
- ☐ **Put Otty on the list first.**
- ☐ Playbooks, stats, bonds, sealed page.
- ☐ Introduce the office: the window, the plate, the chandlery smell, Otty answering the phone.
- ☐ Say the water sentence.
- ☐ Give them a job that pays too well.

---

## THE ELEVEN THINGS TO HOLD

1. Nobody is a villain.
2. The help always works.
3. Never define what the Stand is. Only how it behaves.
4. Violence never closes a case.
5. The licence is the brake, and it belongs to someone kind.
6. The Long Count is a condition, not a conspiracy.
7. Read the list aloud every interlude.
8. Never explain number 11.
9. Say the water sentence every session.
10. Let people leave for ordinary reasons.
11. Don't gloat at the end. This is grief, not punishment.
