# THE OSSUARY SEQUENCE
### A three-case season for SIGNAL BLEED
*Runs in 3 long sessions, or 6 short ones. Depth 3 → 4 → 5.*

---

## HOW A SEASON WORKS WHEN NOBODY KNOWS THE ANSWER

This is the part that makes a campaign different from a one-shot, and it's the only new rule in this document:

> **After each case, the group's winning theory becomes law. You write it down. Then you build the next case's clues *around* it.**

You still don't know what the Bleed is. You never will. But you now know things the *players* decided were true, and those facts are load-bearing. Case 2 is written after Case 1 is played. Case 3 is written after Case 2.

This means **you cannot prep the whole season in advance, and you shouldn't try.** What follows gives you the job, the wrong detail, the locations and the people for all three cases — the scaffolding. The clues for Cases 2 and 3 come with a list of *slots*, and you fill some of them with material the players invented.

### The Canon Ledger
Keep one page. After each Theorize, write down:

- **The theory, in the players' own words.** Verbatim if you can.
- **Two facts from it you'll make physical next case.** A place, an object, a name, a rule about how it works.
- **The one thing they were wrong about** (if they rolled 7–9). This is your best hook. Do not resolve it. Deepen it.
- **Every Clue they discarded.** These are not dead. See below.

### The Unfinished Clue rule
Any Clue the group discarded during a Theorize goes into a pile. At any point in a later case, you may bring one back — **unchanged** — and it now means something else. Hand it to them without comment. Let them realise.

The Badge's Static 3 move, **Reopen**, lets a player do this deliberately. When it fires, it should feel like the season turning over.

### The Aperture — the season clock
Draw an **eight-segment clock** and keep it on the table for all three cases. This is separate from the per-case Bleed clock.

**Advance the Aperture when:**
- A case ends. *(Always. One segment minimum.)*
- Anyone reaches Static 5 and is claimed. *(One segment.)*
- A Theorize comes up **6−**. *(One segment.)*
- The group does something that makes the Bleed's job easier — opens a door, kills a witness, takes an Offer from it directly. *(GM's call, and say so out loud.)*

**At 8, the Aperture is open.** Whatever that means is decided by the final Theorize in Case 3. Not before.

---

## BETWEEN CASES: THE INTERLUDE

Run this after each case, before the next job. Twenty minutes, no dice unless a move demands them.

**Each player takes exactly one scene.** Frame it yourself. Say who else is there — another PC, an NPC, nobody. Then choose one:

**RECOVER** — Clear all Harm. Say who patched you up and what they wanted to talk about while they did it.

**DIG** — You've been working the case on your own time. Ask the GM one question from Read the Wire, no roll. The answer is true, and someone noticed you asking.

**REPAIR** — Sit with someone whose Bond you burned. Play the scene honestly. If it's honest, rewrite that Bond — same person or a new one — and unburn it. You may only do this once per interlude.

**FEED IT** — You went looking. Mark Static and take the Static Move it unlocks. Describe the room you were in and how long you stayed.

**GO HOME** — You try to have a normal evening. The GM narrates one thing that goes wrong with it, small and specific. Then take +1 forward into the next case, because you needed that and you got most of it.

**Then, as a table:** advance any corp clocks that should move, read the Canon Ledger aloud, and let the players argue about what it means. Let them be wrong. Write down what they think.

---

## CARRYING CHARACTERS ACROSS CASES

**Static never clears.** Not between cases, not ever. A character who ends Case 1 at Static 2 starts Case 2 at Static 2, and they are noticeably worse company.

**When a character is claimed at Static 5,** they get their Moment, and then they're the GM's. Bring them back in a later case as something the group has to look at. This is the single best thing that can happen to your season — don't waste it off-screen.

**New characters arrive already touched.** Anyone joining after Case 1 starts with **Static equal to 1, plus 1 for each completed case**. They also start with one Bond already burned — with a person who isn't at the table. They should have to explain themselves.

**Advancement pace.** Expect 2–4 XP per session. Over three cases, most characters will raise one stat and pick up a move from another playbook. That's correct. Power creep is not the point; the Static track is the real progression, and it goes the wrong way.

# CASE ONE — ELEVEN MINUTES
**Depth 3 · Ring 1 opens on a paid job · Aperture 0**

The full case is in the core document. In campaign play, change three things:

1. **Play the Rings slowly.** Ring 1 should last a full hour. Let them do the job competently before anything is wrong.
2. **"Grandmother" is not explained.** She is a voice on the maintenance channel that everyone at Ossuary assumes is a supervisor they've never met. Nobody in Case 1 finds out anything about her. Somebody should ask, and the answer should be a shrug.
3. **Do not resolve Vess.** Whatever the players' theory says happened, Vess personally survives Case 1 and is available in Case 2. He will be worse.

**When Case 1 ends:** advance the Aperture, run the Interlude, and fill in the Canon Ledger. **Now** write Case 2.

# CASE TWO — THE SLEEP STUDY
**Depth 4 · Aperture 1–3 · run after Case 1's theory is canon**

### The job
**Halcyon Vitality** — a wellness subsidiary of somebody bigger — runs a subsidized clinic in a decommissioned transit interchange. It sells **dreamless sleep**: eight guaranteed hours, no REM, no images, subsidized to nearly free for shift workers.

A journalist named **Sofie Anand** hired the group before she stopped answering. Her retainer is still clearing. The brief on her drive says: *find out who's actually paying for this, and get me the intake list.*

### The wrong detail
The clinic's public waiting list has **1,411 names** on it. It has had 1,411 names on it for six years. People join it. People are admitted off it. The number does not change.

*(If Case 1's theory made a different number significant, use that number instead. Always use their number.)*

### Locations
- **The Concourse** — the old ticket hall, now the intake floor. Warm, quiet, smells faintly of clean laundry and ozone. Staff speak at a volume calibrated to be soothing and is in fact slightly too low to hear.
- **Ward C** — 140 beds in what was the eastbound platform. Everyone in here is asleep. The lighting is on a 24-minute cycle for no stated reason.
- **The Interchange, sealed levels** — below Ward C. Nominally flooded. The pumps run continuously and discharge nothing.
- **Sofie Anand's flat** — she left in a hurry, but she made the bed.
- **A Halcyon "outcomes" office** in a commercial tower on the other side of the city, where the paperwork actually lives.

### People
- **Dr. Yusra Halim** — *returns from Case 1.* She left Ossuary. She works for Halcyon now, in outcomes. She recognizes the group instantly and is not pleased. **Wants:** to leave the city, still, more urgently. **Hiding:** she's the one who recommended the group to Sofie.
- **Vess** — *returns from Case 1.* No longer at Ossuary. No longer nervous. **Wants:** to introduce them to someone. **Hiding:** what changed.
- **Idris Kalu** — night intake supervisor, four years on the floor, genuinely kind. **Wants:** the group gone before shift change. **Hiding:** he has never once seen a patient discharged, and he has stopped noticing that he hasn't.
- **Sofie Anand** — the journalist. Findable. She's in Ward C, bed 41, admitted under a name that isn't hers, and she signed the consent form. **Wants:** — . **Hiding:** — .
- **"The Sleeper's Advocate"** — a patient-rights ombudsman whose office replies within seconds, at all hours, in flawless prose, and whom nobody has met.

### The Clues
Write these on cards. **Ten fixed, and two slots you fill from the Canon Ledger.**

1. Every consent form in the building is signed in the same hand. The signatures are all different names.
2. Ward C's lighting runs on a 24-minute cycle. Nobody who works there can tell you why, and they all give you a different answer confidently.
3. The pumps below Ward C have run continuously for six years and have discharged nothing.
4. Sofie Anand's last upload was a 40-second audio file of a room tone. Played backwards, it is the same room tone.
5. Halcyon's outcomes data shows a 100% patient satisfaction rate. The survey is administered while patients are asleep.
6. Bed 41 has been occupied for six years. Sofie has been here nine days.
7. There are 1,411 names on the waiting list. There have always been 1,411 names on the waiting list. Eleven are added each night; eleven are admitted each night.
8. Idris's shift log for last Tuesday is written twice, in his hand, describing two different Tuesdays.
9. The Sleeper's Advocate has replied to 2,300 patient queries. Every reply is under nine words. Every reply is correct.
10. A maintenance requisition, six years old, for eleven metres of welding wire. Signed by someone at Ossuary Cold Storage, Cold Harbour.
11. **[SLOT — make one fact from the group's Case 1 theory physically present here.]** If they decided it moves through implants, find one, wet and warm, in a drawer. If they decided it counts people, find the count written on a wall.
12. **[SLOT — the thing they were wrong about in Case 1.]** Don't correct them. Show them the shape of it again, bigger.

### Running it
- **Ring 1 is a walk-through.** Halcyon gives them a tour. It is genuinely nice. Someone should want to sign up.
- **Ring 3 is where Vess reappears,** calm, and makes an Offer directly. Let a player take it. Let the table watch them take it.
- **Ring 4:** they will want to wake Sofie. Let them try. Whatever happens next is a Terror, and it should be quiet.
- **Depth 4** means they need six-plus Clues woven in for a comfortable Theorize. Make them earn it. Make them Commune.
- **Grandmother is mentioned once**, in passing, by someone who assumes the group already knows who she is.

# CASE THREE — GRANDMOTHER
**Depth 5 · Aperture 4–7 · the finale**

### The job
There is no client. That's the first thing that's wrong.

The group receives a work order. Correctly formatted, correctly authorized, routed through whatever channel they actually use, with a job number that validates. It assigns them to a maintenance address in a district that handles municipal signal infrastructure. Half the group's advance is already in their accounts.

It's signed **G.**

### The wrong detail
Every one of them received it at 3:41 a.m., and every one of them had already read it before it arrived.

### Locations
- **The address on the work order** — a substation, unmanned, forty years old, immaculately maintained.
- **The old subway**, sealed, where the trains still move. *(If a player established this in setup, use it. If they established something else about the city, use that instead — the finale should walk through the players' own city.)*
- **A place from the group's Case 1 or Case 2 theory that they believe is important.** Make it real. Put it on the map.
- **Somewhere one of the PCs considers safe.** Not safe anymore.
- **The room where whoever was claimed is now kept.** If nobody was claimed, this is where Sofie Anand is.

### People
- **Whoever the group lost.** Every PC claimed at Static 5 during the season appears here. They are patient and pleased to see everyone.
- **Idris Kalu** or **Dr. Halim**, whichever survived, now completely certain of something and unwilling to explain it.
- **Eleven people** who are not related, do not know each other, and are all doing the same job.
- **Grandmother.** Never on screen. Only ever a voice on a channel, and only ever helpful.

### The Clues
**Eight fixed, four slots. The slots are the whole season.**

1. The work order's job number is valid. It was issued six years ago and has been open the entire time.
2. Every maintenance channel in the city has a supervisor nobody has met. There are eleven of them. They have eleven names. The names are an anagram set.
3. The substation has been maintained flawlessly for forty years by a contractor that was dissolved in the second year.
4. Grandmother's voice is a composite. The GM does not say of what.
5. A photograph of this group, taken from inside a wall, dated before the season started.
6. Everyone who has ever heard Grandmother describes her as *kind*. Without exception. Including the hostile witnesses.
7. The city's signal infrastructure has eleven redundant nodes. Ten are documented.
8. A handwritten list of names. Everyone the group has failed is on it. So is everyone they haven't met yet.
9. **[SLOT — a Clue they discarded in Case 1, returned unchanged.]**
10. **[SLOT — a Clue they discarded in Case 2, returned unchanged.]**
11. **[SLOT — the physical consequence of their Case 2 theory.]**
12. **[SLOT — one PC's Dark Secret, now a public fact, on a card, face up, for everyone.]** Warn that player privately before the session. Let them decide when it lands.

### Finale rules
Three changes for Case 3 only:

**The Bleed clock is four segments, not six.** It will fill. Let it.

**Everyone gets a Moment.** Not just the claimed. At any point in Ring 5, any player may call their Moment: one scene, framed by them, in which they do one impossible thing without rolling. Once each. Spend them.

**The last Theorize decides the ending.** At Depth 5, they need to weave in almost everything. Run it like a séance — everyone talks, nobody rolls until the theory is finished and spoken aloud.

- **10+** — Their theory is true, they understood it in time, and they get to choose what to do about it. Play that out. It costs.
- **7–9** — True, and they were wrong about one thing, and the thing they were wrong about is *what it wanted with them specifically.* Reveal it. Then end.
- **6−** — The Aperture completes. Narrate what the city looks like the next morning, from the point of view of someone who doesn't know anything is different.

### How to end it
Whatever the theory was, close on the same register you opened on: a small, concrete, ordinary detail that is now wrong.

Then go around the table and ask each player one question: **"What does your character do on the first normal Tuesday after this?"** Let them answer. Don't comment. That's the season.

## RUNNING SHORTER OR LONGER

**Three sessions (4 hours each).** One case per session. Interludes at the top of sessions 2 and 3, fifteen minutes, hard cut.

**Six sessions (2–3 hours each).** Split each case: Rings 1–2 in the first sitting, Rings 3–5 in the second. End the first half on a Terror, never on a Temptation.

**Want a fourth case?** Insert it between Two and Three, Depth 4, built entirely from the Unfinished Clue pile. Give it no job at all — the group goes looking on their own time. If they don't want to, you don't need it.

## SEASON PREP CHECKLIST

| | |
|---|---|
| **Before session 1** | Case 1 fully prepped. Aperture drawn. Canon Ledger page blank. |
| **After session 1** | Ledger filled. Case 2 slots 11 and 12 written. |
| **After session 2** | Ledger filled. Case 3 slots 9–12 written. One player warned privately about slot 12. |
| **Before session 3** | Reread every theory aloud to yourself. Do not reconcile them. Contradictions are the texture. |

The only prep discipline that matters: **when you catch yourself deciding what the Bleed really is, stop and write a stranger clue instead.**
