The core roll

When a move tells you to, roll 2d6 + a stat. Never otherwise.

10+You do it. Clean.
7–9You do it, but — a cost, a complication, a choice.
6−The GM says what happens instead. It's worse than you thought.

You don't roll to see if something is interesting. You roll to see what it costs.

The stats

EDGE — speed, violence, nerve.
WIRE — machines, systems, intrusion.

FACE — reading people, leverage, lying well.
VEIL — the part of you that notices what shouldn't be there.

Basic moves

Push Through (+EDGE)
Act with force, speed or nerve under real threat.
10+You do it, and you're clear. 7–9You do it, but choose one: you take Harm · you leave something behind · someone sees you do it.
Read the Wire (+WIRE or +VEIL)
Study a place, a system, or a person for what's underneath. Say which stat and why.
10+Ask two. 7–9Ask one. 6−Ask one anyway — then the GM asks what you're now afraid of, and you answer honestly.
What here is a lie?
What's about to go wrong?
What time is it really?
Who has already been here?
What here doesn't belong to this world?
Concrete and unexplained? Take a Clue.
Work Someone (+FACE)
Lean on them with leverage, charm, or a very good lie.
10+They give you what you want. 7–9Most of it, and one: they want something first · they'll remember this · they tell someone. 6−They were already working you.
Jack In (+WIRE +STATIC)
Interface with a system that shouldn't exist.
10+You get what you came for. Take a Clue. 7–9You get it, but choose one: mark Static · something on the other side knows your name now · advance the Bleed. 6−Mark Static. The GM says what came back through with you.
Take a Sounding (+VEIL +STATIC)
Open yourself deliberately. Once per scene, no more.
10+Take a Clue and ask one question from Read the Wire. 7–9Take a Clue. Mark Static. 6−Mark Static, and it answers a question you didn't ask.
Theorize (+Clues − Depth)
Name what's really happening. Anyone can build the theory; one person rolls.
10+Your theory is true. It is now what happened. 7–9True, but you were wrong about one thing — and it's the thing that matters. Everyone marks Static. 6−Wrong, and the truth is worse. The Bleed fills. Theorize again later with new Clues.
THE OFFER. Anyone at the table can offer a specific complication for +1 to a roll about to happen. Take it or refuse it, freely. If you take it, the complication happens whether you succeed or not.
"+1 if the cameras get your face."
"+1 if you have to leave her behind."

End of session

Did we learn something true about the Bleed? Everyone marks XP. · Did someone act on their Dark Secret in the open? They mark XP. · Did anyone burn a Bond? They mark XP.

Three XP: raise a stat by +1 (max +3) · take a move from another playbook · heal a burned Bond by making it about someone new.

GM Screen

Signal Bleed
Sheet 9 of 9 · v0.1
You do not know the answer either. Keep it that way.

Agenda

  • Make the city feel bought and sold.
  • Make the unknown genuinely unknown — including to you.
  • Be a fan of these people, right up until they're gone.
  • Play to find out what it costs them.

Principles

  • Ask questions and build on the answers.
  • Address the character, not the player.
  • Sensory detail over exposition. Never explain the horror — describe what it does to a room.
  • Name every NPC and give them one want.
  • Never decide what's really happening. Decide what's strange.
  • When they ask what it is, show them what it did.
  • Everything is owned by someone.

Your moves

On a 6−, or whenever they look at you.

  • Reveal an unwelcome truth.
  • Show a sign of the Bleed.
  • Take something away — a contact, a safehouse, a body part.
  • Put someone innocent in it.
  • Make an Offer.
  • Advance a corp clock, and say how they noticed.
  • Have it acknowledge them. By name.
  • Announce off-screen badness.

Signs of the Bleed (d6)

1Every clock in the room agrees, and all of them are wrong by the same amount.
2The surf stops. Not quietens — stops. For as long as it takes to notice.
3Standing water is tilted, very slightly, toward something.
4A count comes out at eleven when there are nine of you.
5Someone repeats a thing you thought thirty seconds ago — and apologises.
6A door that is on the plan and has never been there.

The five rings

You cannot advance a Ring until you've landed at least one Terror and one Temptation in it.

1 · NormalA job. Money, a client, a deadline. Exactly one detail is wrong, and nobody comments on it.
2 · First SignThe wrong detail isn't isolated. Something is repeating.
3 · EscalationPeople start lying to them specifically. Something is now interested in the group.
4 · RevelationThe rules are visibly broken. Theorize usually happens here.
5 · DissolutionConsequences. Who is claimed. What the city doesn't remember by morning.

The Bleed clock

Advance on: a missed roll that touched the other side · a 7–9 choice that says so · moving into a new Ring · any hard move you want made visible.

When it fills, it arrives. Not a monster — an event. The rules of the scene change. Something becomes true that cannot be untrue. Decide what, and don't hedge.

Writing clues

A Clue is a hole in the world, not a piece of a puzzle you've already solved. Concrete, strange, non-explaining. Eight to twelve per case, on cards, face up on the table.

GOOD — The building's water usage tripled six months ago. It has no tenants.

BAD — The cult has been living in the basement. (explains itself)

GOOD — Her last message was sent four minutes after the crash. It's a photo of the crash.

BAD — She had precognitive abilities. (that's a theory, not a clue)

Prep, twenty minutes maximum

  • A job — who's hiring, the deliverable, the deadline.
  • One wrong detail. Never explain it.
  • Four or five locations: a name, a smell, one person there.
  • Five to seven NPCs: name, want, secret. Two are lying about unrelated things.
  • Eight to twelve Clues on cards.
  • Depth 3.
  • No solution. If you have one in your head, throw it out — you'll steer toward it.
