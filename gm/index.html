Hawaiʻi — Emergency Republic

Fictional alternate history beginning September 2026. Choose the scenario date before revealing later timeline events.

Run it

  • Start with an ordinary job and one discrepant record.
  • Track who knew the warning before the intervention.
  • Show the people affected outside the dashboard.
  • Keep the cosmic source unknown. Let Theorize establish case truths.

Five rings

Normal → First Sign → Escalation → Revelation → Dissolution. Land a Terror and a Temptation before advancing.

Clocks

Bleed: six segments per case; use existing move triggers. Aperture: eight segments across the season; advance at case end, a claimed character, failed Theorize or a disclosed GM trigger. It measures intrusion, not an island count.

People and obligations

Keep specific names and debts. A shared childhood is optional. The finale follows play; no mandatory evacuation or hidden conversion of Bonds into boat seats.

Basic moves

Special

Anyone at the table may offer a specific complication in exchange for +1 to a roll about to happen. Take it or refuse it freely. If taken, the complication happens whether you succeed or not.

"+1 if the fictional contractor logs your badge."
"+1 if you go back down without a tender."

Open with a concrete local condition or displaced cost. “The delivery is on time; the clinic has no driver.” The move name is retained; inevitable flooding is not.

When you catch your character having been helpful in a way you can't account for, name it out loud at the table. Mark XP.

If you don't say it and the GM notices first, mark Static instead.

Confession is protective. Silence compounds.

You lose the character. You keep the voice. Roll up someone new; when the GM decides your claimed character appears, you play them and you decide what help they offer.

The help has to be real. Concrete, correct, generous. No riddles.

Static

0–5, add to VEIL and anything touching the other side. Take a Symptom each mark. Static Moves unlock at 1, 3 and 5. It never clears. At 5 the character is observed — one final scene, one impossible thing, no roll, then the GM has them.

Burning a Bond stops one Static mark. Two Bonds each, once apiece. No hidden evacuation-seat cost applies.

Symptoms (d6)

1You are eleven minutes ahead of every clock on the island, and you can prove it.
2You've stopped hearing the surf. It's still there. Everyone else hears it.
3Standing water tilts slightly toward you.
4You remember an unobserved branch. It hurts to hold.
5There's a day missing and you can feel the edge of it, like a seam.
6Someone has started calling you by a name nobody gave you, and correcting themselves, and doing it again.

Rolled a duplicate? It gets worse. Say how.

Signs of the Signal (d6)

1Every clock in the room agrees, and all of them are wrong by the same amount.
2The surf stops. Not quietens — stops. For as long as it takes to notice.
3Standing water is tilted, very slightly, toward something.
4A count comes out at eleven when there are nine of you.
5Someone repeats a thing you thought thirty seconds ago — and apologises.
6A door, a stair, a corridor that is on the plan and has never been there.

The Signal and The Sounding

A record can describe a possible branch. Reading it can change what follows.

Observation is not neutral. Intervention may prevent a local event while moving its cost elsewhere. Helpful information can be partial without being meaningless.

Keep The Sounding as the larger cosmic unknown. Its exact event and date are not fixed. Grandmother remains an unexplained voice.

Never pre-write the solution

Record established facts, warnings and interventions separately. A successful Theorize establishes case truths; it does not erase the accepted chronology. Do not make every success secretly worthless.

Twenty minutes, maximum

  • A job — who's hiring, the deliverable, the deadline. It pays too well.
  • One wrong detail. Never explain it.
  • Four or five locations: a name, a smell, one person there.
  • Five to seven people: name, want, secret. Two are lying about unrelated things.
  • Eight to twelve clues on cards.
  • Five terrors, five temptations — one pair per ring.
  • Depth 3.
Do not write a solution. If you have one in your head, throw it out — you'll steer toward it.

Writing clues

A clue is a hole in the world, not a piece of a puzzle you already solved.

GoodBad
The tower's water usage tripled after the last tenant left.People are living in the flooded lobby. (explains itself)
Her last transmission logged four minutes after the vessel went down. It describes the vessel going down.She could see the future. (that's a theory, not a clue)

Theorize

Roll 2d6 + clues woven in − depth. Depth 3 one-shot · 4 longer case · 5 finale.

10+Their theory is true. It is now what happened.
7–9True, but wrong about the one thing that matters. Everyone marks Static.
