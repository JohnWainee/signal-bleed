# Default 2050 lifepath: Career pack

**Status:** sponsor-approved content draft for playtest. Career occurs during first work in the brittle Emergency Republic of the early 2040s. Do not author Incident or Fallout and do not implement runtime code without separate authorization.

## Fixed phase contract

Career uses the accepted lifepath procedure: choose one of six concrete job roles, choose and detail one of three role-specific connections, receive three random event families from a pool of six, choose one, roll once, and select A, B, a GM-approved structured custom response, or the same-band safety fallback.

The role records what kind of work the character did. It supplies a persistent history tag and changes event framing, but grants no numeric bonus, assigns no playbook, and restricts no later playbook choice. The player supplies the character's specific title, employer type, and ordinary duties without establishing an unapproved 2040s institution.

## Approved Career tone

Every Career event cell must include:

- a formative crisis in which failure risks death, disappearance, or irreversible loss;
- one concrete impossible detail rooted in ordinary work;
- one person or place pulled into immediate danger;
- one specific callback that can return during Incident or Fallout; and
- no claim about what caused the anomaly or which branch is ultimately real.

Both responses must work locally and save someone or something that matters; the lasting consequence shows what the intervention displaced rather than making the success false. The approved response axes and effect budgets remain. Cells drafted before this tone decision are mechanical scaffolds only until their prose is revised and reapproved. The Handoff is intentionally lower-intensity because it is the safety fallback.

## Sponsor-approved role slate

1. Medical Professional
2. Enforcement Officer
3. Construction Worker
4. Program Administrator
5. Forecast Auditor
6. Route Runner

The first four provide familiar occupational anchors. Forecast Auditor and Route Runner expose the late Emergency Republic's prediction records and inconsistent access systems without deciding whether a forecast, route, branch, or record is cosmically true.

## Sponsor-approved event-family slate

All six roles draw from one shared pool. Each event is reframed through the selected job, its framing cue, and its chosen connection.

1. **The Impossible Brief** — The task cannot meet every stated requirement.
2. **The Revised Record** — The official account changes after the character's work.
3. **The Personal Request** — Someone asks the character to use professional access for them.
4. **The Divided Team** — Two groups claim authority over the same work.
5. **The Cost Moved Elsewhere** — The character's solution works here but harms another queue or place.
6. **The Open Position** — Advancement comes with an unresolved responsibility.

### Approved event axes

- **The Impossible Brief:** A honors the stated priority; B rewrites the scope to protect the need the brief ignored. Neither response solves every requirement.
- **The Revised Record:** A challenges the changed official record openly; B preserves both versions and keeps working without declaring either one false.
- **The Personal Request:** A uses professional access and becomes personally accountable; B protects the boundary but secures another workable route. Both responses help the requester.
- **The Divided Team:** A unites both groups under shared command; B splits the danger into separate responsibilities with a hard boundary.
- **The Cost Moved Elsewhere:** A shuts down the successful intervention and spreads the danger back across the system; B keeps it working and personally carries rescue to the place receiving the displaced cost. Both responses save lives.
- **The Open Position:** A accepts the title and uses its authority to stop the crisis; B refuses the title but takes responsibility for the dangerous work outside its chain of command.
- **The Handoff safety fallback:** A transfers the work face-to-face and remains personally connected; B builds a documented relay so no single person carries it alone.

### Approved event cells

#### The Impossible Brief — Pressure

The brief contains a casualty roster stamped for the end of the character's shift. The chosen connection is listed as dead if the official priority succeeds. If that priority is abandoned, a larger site will fail within the hour.

- **A — Complete the priority and extract the connection personally.** Both survive, but the connection's workplace or home is condemned. Gain the future-dated emergency order. They ask the character to recover one named person or irreplaceable item left behind.
- **B — Rewrite the operation around an emergency evacuation.** Everyone gets out, but the official site is permanently lost. Gain the field plan. An inquest already contains testimony placing the character inside after the evacuation.

#### The Impossible Brief — Entanglement

Another worker brings a second casualty roster. Each plan says the chosen connection survives only if the worker following the other plan dies. Calls from the connection are arriving from both sites.

- **A — Follow the official priority and split the response.** Secure the designated site; the other worker brings the connection out, but one member of their crew disappears. Gain the worker as a contact and owe them the search. The missing person's credential later logs entry at both sites.
- **B — Reject both plans and order a full evacuation.** Everyone physically escapes, but both sites are permanently closed. Gain the joint evacuation plan. The system records the worker and the connection among the dead, and the character must help them live under that contradiction.

#### The Impossible Brief — Room

Days before the operation, the character finds its sealed after-action report dated next month. It lists mass casualties, praises the character for following the brief, and includes the chosen connection's final call.

- **A — Force an official evacuation and funded backup.** Everyone named survives, but the site is sealed permanently. Gain the authorization. Calls from inside continue, and the character remains responsible for answering them.
- **B — Build and test an alternate operation.** Everyone survives and the site remains usable. Gain the procedure. The report rewrites itself with the character as the sole casualty, and their professional identity begins failing verification.

#### The Revised Record — Pressure

During an active rescue, the official roster rewrites itself. The chosen connection and their crew are marked dead, so their treatment, shelter, or evacuation access shuts down. The character's saved copy lists them alive somewhere else. Both versions carry the character's signature.

- **A — Challenge the revision openly and force access back on.** The crew survives. Gain the objection receipt. The connection remains legally alive, but the character is investigated as the source of the false record.
- **B — Treat both versions as provisional and send help to both locations.** The crew survives, and responders find one additional person no current system recognizes. Gain the comparison file. The character becomes responsible for keeping that person alive and hidden from automatic removal.

#### The Revised Record — Entanglement

Another worker has evacuated people named in the revised roster. The character's original now says those evacuees died last week—and that the worker caused it. The chosen connection's group is still trapped because the system says evacuation capacity was already used.

- **A — Join the worker in an open challenge and force a second evacuation.** Everyone escapes. Gain the worker as a contact. Both credentials are suspended, and later footage shows the vehicles leaving with empty seats.
- **B — Keep the records separate and move both groups under different authorizations.** Everyone escapes. Gain the dual-record route. The worker must continue under a different identity and believes the character decided which version of their life could remain public.

#### The Revised Record — Room

Days before the operation, the character finds two certified casualty lists. One says the chosen connection dies; the other says everyone at the worksite dies. Each list updates when someone is warned, but the total number of deaths never changes.

- **A — Build an audit trail and force official evacuation of both predicted sites.** Everyone survives. Gain the audit file. The connection becomes the public source—and the list begins following them to each new address.
- **B — Preserve both versions and build defenses for both outcomes.** Everyone survives and both sites remain open. Gain the controlled archive. At each reconciliation window, one site disappears from services, and the character must carry the proof that keeps its people recognized.

Neither response explains why the record changed.

#### The Personal Request — Pressure

The chosen connection brings a recording of the character's voice telling them to use the character's professional access now. Behind the locked boundary, people will die within minutes. The access log already says the character and connection entered yesterday.

- **A — Use the access directly and complete the rescue.** Everyone survives. Gain the signed emergency entry. The log never records the character leaving, and later systems sometimes treat them as still inside.
- **B — Bring in a trusted professional through another route.** Everyone survives. Gain that person as a contact and owe them one life-saving reciprocal task. Their body camera shows the character directing the rescue from the sealed side.

#### The Personal Request — Entanglement

Another worker receives a recording of the chosen connection begging them to keep the character out. The character's recording says they must enter. Both credentials are required to unlock the rescue route.

- **A — Enter together and complete the rescue.** Everyone survives. Gain the worker as a contact. Afterward, both credentials carry the same biometric record, and systems sometimes treat the two workers as one person.
- **B — Create a formal transfer and send the worker in with combined authority.** Everyone survives. Gain the referral path. Responders recover a body carrying the worker's credentials while the worker is standing beside the character.

#### The Personal Request — Room

Days early, the chosen connection brings a death notice for themselves and several others, signed by the character in their professional role. It describes a preventable disaster behind a boundary only the character's work can open.

- **A — Sponsor a documented rescue in the character's own name.** Everyone survives. Gain the renewable authorization. It now activates at the same time each week with live requests from people who are not there, and the character must personally review every use.
- **B — Build a legitimate independent route with the connection.** Everyone survives. Gain the route guide. The public record names the connection as the operation's sole organizer—and adds one unknown survivor to every copy.

#### The Divided Team — Pressure

Two teams enter the same lethal emergency from different sides. Each radio channel is already playing the other team's final distress call, eleven minutes early. The chosen connection is trapped where their routes cross.

- **A — Merge both teams under one command.** Rescue everyone and gain the combined crew as a contact. The final roster contains one extra responder nobody recognizes, and both teams insist that person saved them.
- **B — Divide the site and run two separate rescues.** Everyone survives. Gain the signed boundary plan. Cameras show the connection leaving by both routes, but only one version reaches the character.

#### The Divided Team — Entanglement

Each team arrives with a different living version of the same missing worker tied to the chosen connection. Each version carries injuries from the disaster currently unfolding at the other team's site.

- **A — Unite the teams and use both workers' knowledge.** Rescue everyone and gain the combined crew as a contact. At final headcount, one version is missing, but both credentials keep transmitting.
- **B — Keep the teams separate and let each worker lead their own rescue.** Everyone survives. Gain the division protocol. Both workers remain alive, but official reconciliation will erase one, and the character must keep both recognized.

#### The Divided Team — Room

Days before the emergency, each team receives footage of the other team dead at the site. Both recordings came from a camera carried by someone tied to the chosen connection.

- **A — Train and deploy both teams under one command.** Everyone survives. Gain the combined emergency crew. Afterward, every survivor remembers attending the other team's funerals, and the character must maintain the only joint roster.
- **B — Design separate routes and responsibilities with a hard boundary.** Everyone survives. Gain the twin response plans. The boundary becomes permanent: crossing it makes one team disappear from live systems until the character restores their records.

#### The Cost Moved Elsewhere — Pressure

The character's intervention stops a lethal failure at the worksite. At that exact moment, every cleared alarm begins sounding at another place tied to the chosen connection. The predicted deaths transfer one-for-one.

- **A — Shut the intervention down and distribute the danger across the wider system.** Nobody dies, but critical service is permanently reduced everywhere. Gain the shutdown protocol. The character remains responsible for manual safety checks at the connection's place.
- **B — Keep the intervention running and lead an evacuation at the receiving site.** Everyone survives. Gain the emergency route. The evacuated place remains standing, but official systems record it as destroyed, and its people need the character's proof to return.

#### The Cost Moved Elsewhere — Entanglement

A worker at the receiving site activates their own countermeasure. The casualty pattern jumps back to the character's site. Each screen shows the other crew dead, with the chosen connection among them.

- **A — Shut down both interventions and unite the crews in a manual rescue.** Everyone survives. Gain the other crew as a contact. Both sites permanently lose automated protection, and the character must coordinate their recurring joint watch.
- **B — Keep the intervention running and cross with the other worker to evacuate their site.** Everyone survives. Gain the cross-site emergency channel. The worker is recorded dead at the character's site and the connection dead at theirs; the character must keep both recognized.

#### The Cost Moved Elsewhere — Room

Days before activation, every test produces live emergency calls from a different possible receiving site. One caller is tied to the chosen connection. The casualty total moves with each test but never falls.

- **A — Cancel the local solution and build weaker safeguards across every known site.** Everyone survives. Gain the network override. Every site now suffers recurring failures, and the character remains their emergency coordinator.
- **B — Keep the solution and evacuate each receiving site before activation.** Everyone survives. Gain the consequence map. The cost finally lands at a place absent from every map, and voices there begin calling the character for rescue.

#### The Open Position — Pressure

The position's previous holder disappears during an active lethal incident. The system appoints the character immediately and begins issuing orders under their name. Only the position's authority can reach the trapped connection and crew.

- **A — Accept the title and use its access to complete the rescue.** Everyone survives. Gain the role authorization. The missing holder continues sending instructions from inside the system, and the character inherits responsibility for finding them.
- **B — Refuse the title and organize the crew outside its command chain.** Everyone survives. Gain the independent crew as a contact. The position remains occupied by someone using the character's name and issuing orders they must answer for.

#### The Open Position — Entanglement

The character and another worker receive appointment notices for the same position. Each notice says the other died in the incident now unfolding. The rescue authority activates only while one of them is recorded dead.

- **A — Accept the title and use the other worker as a living countersign.** Everyone survives. Gain them as a contact. The system keeps alternating which worker is dead, so they can never both be officially present.
- **B — Both refuse and form an independent command.** Everyone survives. Gain the emergency compact. The vacant position activates under the missing holder's voice and brands the crew as deserters.

#### The Open Position — Room

Days before the incident, the position sends the character a complete handover from its current holder, including video of their death. The holder is still alive and denies making it.

- **A — Accept the title early and use its authority to prepare the rescue.** Everyone survives. Gain the role archive. When the incident ends, the former holder disappears from every record except the chosen connection's memory, and the character inherits the search.
- **B — Refuse the title and help the holder build an independent response network.** Everyone survives. Gain that network. Afterward, records show the character held the position for years, and someone with their voice continues reporting for duty.

#### The Handoff — Pressure

The shutdown is moving block by block. One route remains, and the critical handoff must cross now.

- **A — Escort it through the final route and complete the transfer face-to-face.** Gain the receiving crew as a contact. The character and chosen connection are stranded beyond the closure until they create a return route.
- **B — Build a relay across the remaining access points.** The handoff succeeds. Gain the relay network. Its final team is trapped between closures, and the character owes them an extraction.

#### The Handoff — Entanglement

Another crew has an equally critical handoff moving in the opposite direction. Both need the same last corridor before it seals.

- **A — Exchange escorts and carry each other's responsibility through.** Both handoffs succeed. Gain the other crew lead as a contact. Their names remain linked, and they owe each other one future cross-closure rescue.
- **B — Build a two-way relay through a shared midpoint.** Both handoffs succeed. Gain the relay route. The midpoint becomes an unofficial lifeline, and the character is responsible for keeping it supplied and open.

#### The Handoff — Room: The Walkout

The character learns that an order will shut down essential work and abandon the community tied to the chosen connection. The workforce is ready to break with the institution.

- **A — Lead a public walkout and hand the workplace to its crew.** Essential work continues. Gain the occupied workplace as a place asset. The character is named as the organizer and barred from its official chain of command.
- **B — Distribute the work, tools, and clients among independent crews before everyone leaves.** Essential work continues. Gain the breakaway network. The character becomes responsible for keeping its scattered crews supplied and connected.

## Sponsor-approved safety fallback

### The Handoff

A cascading district shutdown is sealing every route. A critical patient, crew, case, repair, authorization, or delivery must cross the closure before access ends. This fallback is a cinematic disaster sequence without the primary events' identity horror. The three band variants and responses remain to be authored and approved.

## Approved role details

### Medical Professional

- **Scope:** Doctor, nurse, medic, technician, pharmacist, or community-care worker.
- **Framing cue:** Notice who needs care now, what record controls access, and what delay will cost.
- **Person:** Who taught you when to act before authorization arrived?
- **Group:** Which care team trusted you during a shortage?
- **Place:** Where did you keep treating people after its official status changed?

### Enforcement Officer

- **Scope:** Patrol officer, investigator, transport security, emergency-compliance officer, or contracted guard.
- **Framing cue:** Notice who is subject to an order, what evidence supports intervention, and who bears the consequences.
- **Person:** Who taught you to question an order without abandoning the people affected?
- **Group:** Which unit or partner trusted your account when systems disagreed?
- **Place:** Which post, route, or checkpoint made you responsible for the same people every day?

### Construction Worker

- **Scope:** Laborer, carpenter, electrician, equipment operator, inspector, or site supervisor.
- **Framing cue:** Notice what the plans omit, who depends on the structure, and what will fail first.
- **Person:** Who taught you which shortcuts never to take?
- **Group:** Which crew kept working when plans changed overnight?
- **Place:** Which unfinished or repeatedly repaired site did you know better than its records?

### Program Administrator

- **Scope:** Analyst, scheduler, procurement worker, case manager, manager, or contractor coordinator.
- **Framing cue:** Notice the approval chain, the metric driving it, and whose real need the process cannot see.
- **Person:** Who taught you how decisions were actually made?
- **Group:** Which office trusted your judgment when the dashboard and local reports conflicted?
- **Place:** Which office, archive, or meeting room held the version everyone really used?

### Forecast Auditor

- **Scope:** Review analyst, warning assessor, model auditor, records examiner, or independent reviewer.
- **Framing cue:** Notice what was predicted, who knew it, and where an intervention moved the cost.
- **Person:** Who taught you to separate a warning from an established event?
- **Group:** Which review team trusted you to preserve a dissenting reading?
- **Place:** Where were discredited or superseded forecasts kept instead of destroyed?

### Route Runner

- **Scope:** Courier, driver, cargo handler, transport escort, deckhand, or access runner.
- **Framing cue:** Notice which route works in practice, what custody record follows it, and who may be stranded at the next handoff.
- **Person:** Who taught you to read the route instead of trusting the schedule?
- **Group:** Which runners shared live route and access information?
- **Place:** Which handoff point kept accepting your deliveries after it disappeared from the official schedule?
