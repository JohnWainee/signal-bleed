# Default 2050 lifepath: Origin pack

**Status:** sponsor-approved after the Origin desk playtest and six recorded revisions. An observed table playtest remains future evidence. Do not implement runtime code or author Career, Incident, or Fallout without separate authorization.

## Origin procedure

1. Choose one of six backgrounds.
2. Choose and detail one of its three connection prompts.
3. Randomly receive three of the six event families and choose one.
4. Roll unmodified 2d6: 2–6 pressure, 7–9 entanglement, 10–12 room to manoeuvre.
5. Choose A, B, an approved structured custom response, or the same-band safety fallback.
6. Confirm a private receipt containing background, connection, event/band, response, asset, and lasting consequence. Reveal only an optional one-line public version.

Each background supplies a persistent tag and callback, not a numeric bonus or playbook restriction. Every response resolves the immediate situation, grants one document/contact/access/place asset, and creates a lasting relationship, obligation, exposure, or unfinished question.

Before confirmation, every obligation must name who can call it in and one concrete thing they may ask the character to do. If the table cannot name both, record a relationship complication, exposure, or unfinished question instead of a vague debt.

Every structured custom response must also carry one real lasting cost: an obligation, exposure, unresolved question, or relationship complication. A beneficial relationship facet may accompany that cost but cannot replace it.

Receipt templates:

- **Private:** “You grew up in [background]. [Connection answer]. During [event], you [response]. You can call on [asset]. [Lasting consequence].”
- **Public, only when revealed:** “[Character] came from [background] and still carries a connection from [event].”

Structured custom-response worksheet:

1. What does the character do?
2. What real risk or cost do they accept?
3. Who or what is involved?

The GM drafts an immediate resolution, exactly one document/contact/access/place asset, exactly one lasting cost, and the private/public receipts. Compare it with A and B at the rolled band, edit it to the same capability budget, and approve all effects before confirmation.

## Backgrounds and connections

### Neighborhood Network

**Framing cue:** Notice who a shared record leaves out; risk your standing with the people who rely on it when you take responsibility.

- Person: Who vouched for you when a record was wrong?
- Group: Which household or crew gave you your first responsibility?
- Place: Where did everyone check who had made it home?

### Harbor Corridor

**Framing cue:** Notice the real schedule, custody chain, and shift change; risk someone's work or passage when you intervene.

- Person: Who could read the real schedule when the posted one was wrong?
- Group: Which shift crew or row of vendors treated you as one of theirs?
- Place: Where did you wait when arrivals and departures stopped matching?

### Maintenance Community

**Framing cue:** Notice early failure and the workaround keeping things usable; risk inheriting responsibility for the repair or access.

- Person: Who taught you how to notice something failing early?
- Group: Which repair crew let you help with small jobs?
- Place: What piece of infrastructure felt like part of your home?

### Threshold Commuter

**Framing cue:** Notice where systems disagree about a person or crossing; risk linking identities, credentials, or routes when you help.

- Person: Who helped you cross when the systems disagreed about you?
- Group: Which regular travellers learned to watch out for one another?
- Place: Which checkpoint, station, or waiting area became a second home?

### Between Addresses

**Framing cue:** Notice where a system assumes one fixed address; risk making a household, helper, or temporary refuge newly visible.

- Person: Who always found room for you?
- Group: Who helped when it was time to move again?
- Place: Where could messages and deliveries reliably find you?

### The Old Grid

**Framing cue:** Notice where old procedure and current authority conflict; risk a traceable visit or later review when you use what still works.

- Person: Who taught you the old procedures that still worked there?
- Group: Which office, school, or residents' group maintained its own records?
- Place: Which entrance always recognized you—and recorded every visit?

## Event families

### The Missing Name

A person tied to the chosen connection is absent from an essential service list. Adapt the service to the background: transport, supplies, school, maintenance access, housing, or checkpoint clearance.

**Pressure:** Service is closing or departing. A worker will help only if someone accepts responsibility now.

- **A — Put your name beside theirs.** They receive the service. Gain a co-signed access record. Future questions about their entry also lead to you.
- **B — Request a manual exception.** They receive the service. Keep the exception receipt. The office records that you challenged its list.

**Entanglement:** Someone already tried to correct the list and named you as corroboration. Their access is now tied to yours.

- **A — Confirm the shared correction.** The name is restored. Keep the co-signed correction and strengthen that person's trust. Both of you may be called into a later review.
- **B — Replace it with your own statement.** The name is restored and the other person's access is separated from yours. Keep an attributable statement; the institution now treats you as the sole source.

**Room:** You notice early and have time to compare two conflicting records before anyone is denied service.

- **A — Reconcile the service list.** Submit the supporting record and secure lasting recognition. Keep the corrected entry. Your background connection is now visible as its source.
- **B — Arrange parallel confirmation.** Secure temporary access without changing either record. Keep the confirmation method. The mismatch remains and must be renewed or resolved later.

### The Closed Route

A route tied to the chosen connection closes without consistent notice. It may be transport, harbor access, a maintenance passage, a checkpoint, or entry into the Old Grid.

**Pressure:** Someone is stranded. The immediate choices are an unofficial detour or an attributable emergency passage.

- **A — Take the unofficial detour.** Everyone reaches the other side. Gain the route as a place asset and owe the person who opened it.
- **B — Sign for emergency passage.** Everyone crosses under your responsibility. Keep the authorization as an access asset; the crossing remains attributable to you.

**Entanglement:** A crew can get people through, but only by listing you as part of its ongoing work.

- **A — Join the crew roster.** The crossing succeeds. Gain the crew as a contact and owe a future shift or task.
- **B — Arrange a one-time subcontract.** The crossing succeeds without joining the crew. Keep the work order; its unusual scope may face later review.

**Room:** You receive reliable warning before the closure becomes public. Using or sharing it may reveal your source.

- **A — Issue an attributable warning.** Official channels handle the closure. Keep the advisory. Your source becomes visible and may stop sharing early notices.
- **B — Circulate it through trusted contacts.** Your connected community reroutes safely. Gain the warning network. The notice does not reach everyone and leaves no official explanation.

### The Unclaimed Delivery

An essential delivery arrives for a household or place the system says does not exist. Adapt the contents to the background: supplies, mail, repair parts, medicine, or access credentials.

**Pressure:** The vehicle is leaving. The delivery will be returned unless someone accepts custody immediately.

- **A — Accept custody yourself.** The delivery reaches its intended users. Keep the custody receipt. You are responsible for explaining where it went.
- **B — Create a witnessed handoff.** The delivery reaches its intended users under a local witness record. Gain the witnesses as a contact. The disputed destination becomes known to everyone who signed.

**Entanglement:** Another household or crew has stored it temporarily and now shares responsibility for it.

- **A — Keep the storage group involved.** Continue through shared custody. Gain their storage site as a place. Both groups must help reconcile the next disputed delivery.
- **B — Close their part of the chain.** They transfer everything with a complete storage log. Keep the log. You become solely responsible for the unresolved destination.

**Room:** You receive both the manifest and physical delivery early, giving you time to arrange where it goes.

- **A — Establish a recognized destination.** Create a standing delivery entry and gain it as access. The household or place is now visible to future reviews.
- **B — Establish an alternate drop point.** Route deliveries through a trusted location and gain it as a place. The disputed address remains; every delivery requires coordination.

### The Sudden Vacancy

Someone fails to appear for an important duty, and you are asked to fill in. Adapt the duty to the background: checking households, tracking arrivals, repair watch, escorting a route, coordinating a move, or maintaining records.

**Pressure:** The duty begins immediately. There is no handoff, and a mistake will affect the connected community.

- **A — Continue the missing person's work.** Complete the duty using what they left behind. Gain their work kit or notes and inherit one unfinished responsibility.
- **B — Rebuild the duty around available people.** Complete it through an improvised crew and gain them as a contact. The official record attributes the procedural departure to you.

**Entanglement:** You and another substitute are both assigned. Separate records give each of you full responsibility.

- **A — Work under shared responsibility.** Complete the duty together and gain the other substitute as a contact. Any later review calls both of you back.
- **B — Divide the duty and document the boundary.** Complete separate parts and keep the signed division. Anything crossing between the parts remains unresolved.

**Room:** You receive early warning and access to the missing person's ordinary notes or tools before the duty begins.

- **A — Prepare and take the duty yourself.** Complete it cleanly and gain the role's access. You become the person called when coverage fails again.
- **B — Build a rotating replacement plan.** Organize several people and gain the rotation as a contact. You remain responsible for keeping its roster current.

### The Borrowed Pass

Someone needs a credential—or offers theirs—to cross an ordinary barrier. The pass may cover transport, work access, housing entry, maintenance space, or the Old Grid.

**Pressure:** One transferable pass remains, and access is closing. Its holder must accompany the user or formally transfer it.

- **A — Cross together.** The holder escorts the person through. Gain the holder as a contact. The crossing permanently links both names in the access log.
- **B — Transfer the pass.** The person crosses with the reassigned credential. Gain the pass as access. Its former holder loses access until you help replace it.

**Entanglement:** The pass works, but its holder remains responsible for every use and renewal.

- **A — Keep the sponsorship active.** The pass remains usable and becomes an access asset. Every renewal requires the holder's participation.
- **B — Separate the records.** Secure an independent credential and keep it. The earlier uses enter a formal review.

**Room:** You find a legitimate temporary-access process before the deadline and can decide whose records become linked.

- **A — Use a named sponsor.** Receive renewable guest access and gain the sponsor as a contact. Their record now includes responsibility for your entry.
- **B — Take independent temporary access.** Receive and keep a credential in your own name. It expires after the stated purpose and records every use.

### The Conflicting Message

Two trusted sources give incompatible instructions about an ordinary responsibility. Adapt it to the background: a delivery, repair, crossing, household check, move, or access change.

**Pressure:** People or resources must move now. Following either instruction makes the other impossible.

- **A — Commit to one instruction.** That task is completed. Gain its sender as a contact. The other sender expects an explanation and their task remains open.
- **B — Move everything to a temporary holding point.** Neither instruction is completed, but the people or resources are safe and both tasks remain viable for a short time. Gain the holding point as a place. Both sources demand an answer before that window closes.

**Entanglement:** Two groups have already begun acting on different instructions, and each expects you to coordinate.

- **A — Merge the two efforts.** Both groups complete a combined plan. Gain the combined crew as a contact. They now expect you to coordinate future conflicts.
- **B — Separate and sequence the work.** Both tasks are completed in an agreed order. Keep the routing plan. Future delay between the groups is attributed to your sequence.

**Room:** You receive both messages early enough to preserve them and ask one clarifying question, but not enough to prove which source is correct.

- **A — Build a workable plan from both.** Use the answer without declaring either source false. Keep the annotated messages and inherit the unresolved reconciliation.
- **B — Connect the sources directly.** They agree on an immediate action. Gain the joint channel as a contact. Both know you preserved their conflicting instructions.

## Universal safety fallback: The Last Run

A package, tool, or message must reach its destination before transport closes. A player may select this at the rolled band instead of the primary event, without explanation or penalty.

When safe, make the sender, destination, crew, or recipient part of the character's chosen connection. If that connection is what the player wants to avoid, introduce a neutral new contact or place instead; the player does not need to explain the substitution.

**Pressure:** The final vehicle is leaving now, and the destination lies beyond its normal stop.

- **A — Ride with the final vehicle.** Hand-carry the delivery from its last stop. Gain the vehicle crew as a contact and owe them for taking you beyond the normal route.
- **B — Take the service shortcut.** Deliver through a maintenance or staff route. Gain the shortcut as a place; the access log records your unauthorized timing.

**Entanglement:** Another courier has an urgent delivery and proposes combining the runs.

- **A — Run both deliveries together.** Both arrive. Gain the other courier as a contact. You owe each other one future run.
- **B — Swap the final legs.** Each takes the other's delivery. Gain access to the other courier's route. The custody records permanently cross-link your names.

**Room:** You receive early warning and can prepare either a direct route or a coordinated relay.

- **A — Take the direct route.** Secure fast transport and deliver personally. Gain that transport access. The complete trip is attributable to you.
- **B — Build a relay.** Several people move the delivery in stages. Gain the relay network as a contact. You become responsible for coordinating its next use.

## Playtest stop

Do not author the remaining phases yet. Test whether three dealt event choices feel meaningfully different, backgrounds produce visible framing changes, assets are comparably useful, obligations invite play rather than bookkeeping, custom responses stay within the preset budget, and The Last Run feels like a fair alternative rather than a lesser result.

## Sponsor-approved playtest revisions

1. Added one framing cue to each background so its perspective and risk reliably change how an event is presented without adding a numeric benefit or predetermining the event's truth.
2. Revised Conflicting Message / Pressure B so the temporary holding point preserves both tasks for a short time. It now offers immediate value comparable to completing one task while retaining the complication of answering both sources.
3. Required each obligation to identify who can call it in and one concrete thing they may ask. Vague debts become another explicit lasting hook before confirmation.
4. Required every structured custom response to carry one real lasting cost. A beneficial relationship may accompany the cost but cannot replace it.
5. Tied The Last Run to the chosen connection when safe, with a neutral new contact or place whenever that connection is what the player wants to avoid.
6. Added the three-question structured custom-response worksheet directly to the Origin pack so the complete phase can be run without switching documents.
